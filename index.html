Why the European Union?

450 million consumers. One structured decision.

The European Union combines market scale, regulatory stability and direct access to 450 million consumers across 27 member states, creating long-term commercial opportunity for UK businesses prepared to structure their expansion properly.

The decision to create an EU presence is not solely about market access. It concerns how that presence is structured, how it interacts with UK operations, and how regulatory and tax considerations are managed across jurisdictions.

J. Dauman & Co, our approach

Market Scale

Access to over 450 million consumers across 27 member states within a single, integrated regulatory framework.

Expand for analysis

One set of EU rules on product standards, consumer protection and the free movement of goods applies across all 27 member states. A single EU entity can therefore serve customers from Lisbon to Helsinki without negotiating market access country by country, and without the customs friction UK exporters have faced since 2021.

Post-Brexit Regulatory Divergence

UK and EU rules are diverging across product standards, data law, professional qualifications and financial services – and the gap is widening each year.

Expand for analysis

Each divergence adds compliance cost for businesses selling into the EU from the UK. An EU-based entity lets you work to EU rules directly, with local advisers who track changes as they happen, instead of re-certifying or restructuring every time the two regimes move apart.

Regulatory Stability

Operate within established legal and reporting frameworks that provide contractual certainty and operational predictability across all 27 member states.

Expand for analysis

EU directives and regulations give a common baseline for company law, accounting, VAT and data protection. That predictability makes it easier to plan investment, secure financing and sign long-term contracts with European partners who prefer to deal with an EU counterparty.

Tax Structuring

Several EU member states offer corporate tax rates materially below the UK's 25% standard rate. Structured correctly, the right EU jurisdiction can reduce your effective tax burden significantly – compliantly and permanently.

Expand for analysis

The benefit depends on substance: where decisions are made, where people work and how profits are attributed between the UK and EU entities. We review transfer pricing, double tax treaties and permanent establishment risk before recommending a structure, so the saving holds up under scrutiny from both tax authorities.

Global Trade Platform

An EU entity is not just a gateway to Europe – it unlocks preferential access to over 70 countries through the EU's external trade agreement network.

Expand for analysis

Goods that qualify under EU rules of origin can benefit from reduced or zero tariffs under the EU's agreements with partners such as Japan, Canada, South Korea and Mexico. For businesses with ambitions beyond Europe, an EU base can widen market access rather than duplicate it.

Workforce & Talent Access

An EU-registered entity can hire across all 27 member states without visa sponsorship, unlocking a 220 million-strong workforce on commercially competitive terms.

Expand for analysis

Freedom of movement means an EU entity can recruit EU nationals in any member state on local contracts. Markets such as Poland combine a large pool of skilled, English-speaking professionals with employment costs well below London levels.

Access to Financial Systems

Local banking access and integration within European financial infrastructure, including SEPA payments and EU capital markets.

Expand for analysis

A local EU bank account with an EU IBAN simplifies euro collections and supplier payments, reduces FX and transfer costs, and removes the friction some EU customers face when paying a UK account. It also opens the door to EU lenders, grants and investment programmes.

Strategic Positioning

Establishing a registered presence inside the European regulatory framework changes commercial position from external supplier to internal market participant.

Expand for analysis

Many EU buyers, distributors and public-sector tenders favour or require an EU-registered counterparty. A local entity, local VAT number and local contact point signal long-term commitment and remove a common objection in procurement processes.

Institutional Connectivity

Access to European business chambers, embassy networks and trade institutions that accelerate market entry and provide commercial intelligence.

Expand for analysis

Chambers of commerce, trade agencies and embassy networks provide introductions, sector data and market-entry support. Our liaison with the Polish Embassy and membership of the Chartered Institute of Export & International Trade help clients plug into these networks from day one.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do I need an EU company to trade with European customers?

Not always – it depends on what you sell, to whom and in what volume. Selling cross-border from the UK is often possible, but brings VAT, customs and credibility limits. We assess this case by case before recommending any formal step.

Which EU country should I register in?

It depends on your commercial model, target markets, supply chain and tax position. For most British SMEs, Poland is the most grounded starting point – and it's where we have our own offices.

How long does it take?

Straightforward registrations can be completed in 2–4 weeks. Registered office, VAT setup or multiple jurisdictions take longer – you get a clear timeline in the initial assessment.

Can you handle both my UK and EU accounting?

Yes. As an ACCA-authorised UK firm with presence in Poland, we coordinate both from a single point of accountability.
